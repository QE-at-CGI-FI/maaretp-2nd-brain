# Michael Bolton

Context-driven testing figure (@michaelbolton). Her other main interlocutor
in 2010 on [[../concepts/checking-vs-testing]]. She quotes his line "I test.
You observe. We talk. You decide." approvingly as a certification-scheme
one-liner.

## 2011 — [[../batches/2011|batch]]

Her most frequent single interlocutor this year by volume — long threads on
testing-investment ratios at her workplace, the ethics/framing of
acceptance testing as "not a fixing phase," and (with [[james-bach]]) the
week-long [[juha-itkonen]] dissertation debate. Teaches a Rapid Software
Testing course in Helsinki (attended by her, May 2011) and announces
another for Jan 2012. She actively promotes his courses/talks to the
Finnish community via [[fast|FAST]].

## 2012 — [[../batches/2012|batch]]

Named 2013 EuroSTAR Programme Chair; she's tagged alongside him and
@eviltester in the CFP announcement, a marker of her own growing
international visibility. Their exchanges shift from
[[../concepts/checking-vs-testing]] terminology toward closer
collaboration: he coaches her through the [[testing-research|empirical
research]] she starts doing on her own bug-fix data, recommends a
qualitative-methods book, and she uses his taxi-driver metaphor with her
own developers to explain why testing isn't "the best use of his valuable
time." Also the source of "caring is more important than checking," which
she sits with for weeks.

## 2014 — [[../batches/2014|batch]]

The year's real friction with him: a sustained disagreement over
[[../entities/iso29119|ISO 29119]] — she wants the CDT community to offer
some alternative "standard" or coherent framework rather than nothing, he
rejects the premise ("tell them to go work on writing a programming
standard so that we won't need a testing standard"). Distinct from, and
milder than, the [[james-bach|Bach]] rupture the same autumn — this
disagreement stays collegial. At year's end, he's the person she turns to
to think through test strategy vs. tactics, landing eventually on a chess
metaphor.

## 2015 Q2 — the debate reignites hard — [[../batches/2015-q2|batch]]

The [[../concepts/checking-vs-testing]] vocabulary fight comes back sharper
than any round since 2013 — a sustained multi-day exchange (9–15 June)
covering whether creating checks is itself testing, whether the
tester-as-gatekeeper metaphor holds, and whether she'd change production
code herself. She holds her ground point by point but names a real personal
cost mid-debate: "It's so unnice that I've been seriously considering
stopping work on software. The industry needs to learn to be nicer." Blogs
twice processing it afterward — "on my trouble with checking vs. testing"
and, days later, "on feeling like an idiot with all the word play." Distinct
from the Bach rupture the same month (see [[james-bach]]) — this stays
combative but not personal; she's still explicit she respects him.
