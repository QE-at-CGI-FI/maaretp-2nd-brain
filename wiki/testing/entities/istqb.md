# ISTQB

The certification body she has a persistently critical relationship with —
notable because her name is *on* the foundation syllabus, which makes her
criticism carry particular weight.

## 2011 — [[../batches/2011|batch]]

- A marketing firm contracted by ISTQB identifies her, from Member Board
  contacts, as a possible "detractor" and asks her five questions on why.
  She answers once, calls it a waste of time, won't do it again.
- Still not simply anti-ISTQB: defends giving Bolton/ISTQB board members
  room to speak to [[fast|FAST]] rather than shutting out that viewpoint.
- Runs an informal survey at a FAST session: only 5 of 45 respondents felt
  uncomfortable with ISTQB criticism — less resistance than she expected.
- RTs another Finnish board member's public resignation from the local
  ISTQB board, citing "Stockholm syndrome."
- Frames [[ast-bbst|AST BBST]] as the alternative she'd rather promote.

## 2012 — [[../batches/2012|batch]]

The relationship gets more complicated, not less. At her new job she finds
a consultant's document reusing text she wrote — it's a section of the
**ISTQB Foundation syllabus itself**, which she co-authored; she calls
reuse of it "as much reuse of something I'm not proud of can be a
compliment." Pushes back hard on a friend's casual ISTQB-bashing,
insisting on critical thinking over reflexive hate: "questioning your
knowledge on what ISTQB Foundation Syllabus actually includes that is so
bad. Blind follow?" A colleague separately describes "Stockholm syndrome"
from working with ISTQB (echoing the 2011 board-resignation RT). Matti
Vuori jokingly proposes "NoSTQB," handing out certificates of
*un*certification — she engages with the idea seriously enough to blog a
follow-up on self-certification diplomas.

## 2014 — [[../batches/2014|batch]]

A closing note on the "co-author" thread: "I tried 'improving' the ISTQB
Foundation Syllabus and it's been the experience of most time wasted on
least progress. Politics." Reads as her giving up on reform from within,
years after first discovering her own text still circulating in it. The
[[iso29119|ISO 29119]] fight this year is presented by both sides in
contradictory ways re: its ISTQB linkage — one working-group member
denies any link, another says 29119 is based directly on ISTQB syllabi —
which she notes without resolving.

## 2013 — [[../batches/2013|batch]]

A concrete new wrinkle: reading about Keith Klain's ISTQB petition reminds
her "I have some rights to the foundation syllabus. I never signed the
papers" — she co-authored content she never formally signed away. Floats
"FiSTS" (Finnish Software Testing Skills) as a genuine alternative brand to
stand against "the ISTQB certification marketing crowd," and separately
positions ISTQB course content as "folklore of test-speak" — useful for
jargon literacy, nothing more. Still engages directly rather than just
critiquing from outside: attends a local ISTQB conference "just to see,"
and notes wryly the agenda has no resemblance to the syllabi.

## 2015 Q3 — from reform-from-within to bridge-building — [[../batches/2015-q3|batch]]

A real pivot from 2014's "given up... politics" note, prompted by an
extended debate with a fellow CDT member (@kengel100) and
[[michael-bolton|Bolton]] about whether taking ISTQB's money for a
certificate is a betrayal. She reframes it as pragmatic: "the more CDT
community pushes negatively, the more it alienates people. Let's change
something," and "I do wonders for CDT with ISTQB money" — treating the
~200 EUR cert fee as a funding source for CDT work rather than a
concession. Ends the quarter speaking at **FiSTB Testing Assembly**
(#TAHelsinki), an ISTQB-affiliated conference, calling it "bridges" —
and actively works the room finding new women speakers for future CDT
events, treating the ISTQB crowd as a recruiting ground rather than
opposition.

## 2022 Q3 — why she stopped contributing, named directly — [[../batches/2022-q3|batch]]

A new, concrete detail behind her long-standing "detractor" stance:
"I was there to create ISTQB foundation syllabus. Someone in that group
deleting changes added through change management process was politics
I was not prepared for. So failed at correcting worst of it and has not
contributed since." A public LinkedIn post restating her anti-ISTQB
position gets real reach (300 likes, ~30,000 views), and she separately
flags a specific, current concern — a local ISTQB board member
apparently pressuring a training provider to remove competing (non-
ISTQB) courses — hoping it's "a rogue individual and not something they
drive systematically."
