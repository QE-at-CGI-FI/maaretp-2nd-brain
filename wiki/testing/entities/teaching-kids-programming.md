# Teaching Kids Programming / Hour of Code

An [[agilefinland|AgileFinland]] initiative she builds essentially from
scratch in 2014, reaching real scale by year's end.

## 2014 — [[../batches/2014|batch]]

- March: agrees with her local school's headmaster to run a computer club
  for 1st graders starting that autumn, explicitly inspired by her own
  childhood C64 memories.
- Develops "Teaching Kids Programming" courseware under AgileFinland's
  banner; runs it at multiple schools (Ylä-Malmin peruskoulu among them),
  teaching groups from 1st through 9th grade.
- December: participates in Code.org's global Hour of Code (8–14 Dec) with
  at least three AgileFinland-organized sessions, including one at her own
  daughter's kindergarten (8 girls, 2 boys) — "got to be the cool mom."
  When Code.org's servers buckle under global load on the first day, she
  runs an unplugged backup activity instead.
- Reflects publicly and specifically on the teaching itself, not just the
  logistics — blogs twice on what she's learning from the kids' sessions.

This becomes the concrete form of a value she's stated abstractly for
years (building local testing/tech community); notable that her *own*
household — a daughter old enough to code a game at a friend's, other
kids' parents' feedback — is now part of the same project as her
professional organizing.

## 2015 Q1 — [[../batches/2015-q1|batch]]

Scales further: Llewellyn Falco delivers sessions in Turku (Turku ICT
Week) while she runs her own recurring session with a group of
6-year-olds locally, this round doing unplugged (no-computer) exercises.
AgileFinland launches **#koodikummi** ("code godparent") — a call for
volunteers to mentor kids' coding — under the wider #koodi2016 banner.

## 2015 Q2 — [[../batches/2015-q2|batch]]

Her own kids get directly involved for the first time as participants,
not just an inspiration: they playtest Linda Liukas's **Hello Ruby** with
her, coming away talking about CPU/DPU/RAM/ROM as "inhabitants of the
computer." A small, specific detail: her daughter's self-selected
"remote controller" action in the game is "make mom happy." Separately,
she requests man-woman pairs for kids' teaching sessions specifically "to
model what is right" (see [[../concepts/gender-in-tech]]), and blogs
about deliberately avoiding passing her own biases on to kids given
programming's move into the national curriculum from 2016 — "9 years in
school, an hour at a time adds up."
