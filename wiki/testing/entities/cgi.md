# CGI

## 2024 — her employer from June, Director-level testing leadership — [[../batches/2024|batch]]

Her next employer after `vaisala.md`, joined 3 June 2024 as **Director**,
leading testing services with an explicit AI focus. The company
announces the hire with a press release ("Maaret Pyhäjärvi nimitetty
CGIn johtajaksi") and Finnish IT media covers it too, one headline
dubbing her "testausguru" (testing guru) — titles she finds a little
funny given her self-image as someone who "knows nothing and
experiments with everything to learn." The role is consulting/
contracting rather than product work: multiple concurrent customers,
a target of roughly 130 customer meetings a year, and building her own
team from scratch (dozens of applications reviewed per open position).
She negotiated, and got approved, the ethical groundwork for using
GitHub Copilot on production code — risk assessment, tool-version
upgrades, and three compensations: public transparency about the
experience, time invested in open source, and money donated to
sustaining open-source projects. The job also brings her back to public
speaking (see `international-speaking.md`) after her 2023 retirement,
starting with a talk the day after she joined.

## 2025 — the business of testing services, made explicit — [[../batches/2025|batch]]

Year two turns concrete on what "director of testing services" actually
means as a business, not just a title — see
`../concepts/management-transition.md` for the same year's burnout and
title-fight arc. She names her real split of time directly: "manager of
a team of max 20 people (20%), business developer/sales person of
everything testing (35%), consultant delivering quality-related
projects/improvements (37%), competence developer of everything testing
(8%)" — and that clients propose cutting the last two first, the ones
she'd be unhappy losing. Sits through mandatory sales training and
dislikes nearly all of it ("I hate almost every advice I get in sales
training. What is wrong with me?"), while still reframing "sales" on her
own terms — the work that gets invoiced indirectly in the margins. Adds
two more ISTQB Advanced certifications to complete the full set of four,
plus **CPACC** (accessibility), not convinced either credential taught
her much but useful as a "sales training" for testers and a hiring
signal she'd otherwise dismissed. A first-year annual report and FY25
numbers post (62 client organizations worked with) both read as her own
audit of a job whose scope keeps outrunning any single title. Runs a
weekly Python-for-testers course to ~200 colleagues and pushes a
recurring "contemporary exploratory testing" pitch internally, alongside
guiding the org's testing-services model toward **product owning
testing** rather than a separate testing team.

## 2026 — a 2025 audit, a reorg, and "three jobs" — [[../batches/2026|batch]]

Opens the year auditing 2025's hours: 39% paid client work, 32%
*unpaid* client work, 20% internal management, plus training and
sick time — "the idea that I am paid *more* when customers make me do
more unpaid work does not match my ideas of fair." January brings a
real testing-service reorg: a 16-person team split into 5+11, the
11 handed to another cost center with a promised cut of 2 that lands
at 5. She names the arrangement's business logic plainly by June: two
transformation projects she drove let her invoice 30% and 20% of her
hours respectively, "in-org invoicing," meaning the client likely paid
nothing directly for either — "my existence is both good business
(enabling others) and bad business (so much free work)." Real, costed
wins keep landing anyway: a €25k/year bug catch, a 30%/4.65-FTE
saving, a 9.28/10 client feedback score on the day she returns from a
four-week vacation, a QA-services framework-agreement bid win covered
in a trade magazine. She sums up the whole arrangement as "I have
effectively three jobs, and I am competent in one of them, failing for
lack of time constraints in another, and failing for just being bad at
one" — naming sales as the one she's bad at and, increasingly, refuses
to apologize for: declining unpaid "just in case" bid work, and
turning down a demanding, well-matched external role because it would
require a 25% pay cut from her current one.
