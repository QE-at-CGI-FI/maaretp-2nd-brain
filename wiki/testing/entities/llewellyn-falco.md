# Llewellyn Falco

Co-author with her of the **Mob Programming Guidebook** (self-published
via Leanpub, first shipped [[../batches/2015-q4|2015 Q4]]) — the
previously-unnamed co-author referenced throughout
[[../concepts/whole-team-testing]]'s Mob Programming Guidebook thread
since its first publication. Identified by name in the archive for the
first time only in [[../batches/2019-q4|2019 Q4]], in the context of a
serious falling-out.

## 2019 Q4 — a copyright dispute, doxxing, and a mass block — [[../batches/2019-q4|batch]]

A copyright/piracy dispute over the Mob Programming Guidebook escalates
sharply in late October. She lays out a timeline publicly: a DMCA
takedown request removes the book from Leanpub; an illegal copy appears
on GitHub; she requests a takedown there (7 Apr); the response is a
copyright filing with a **US patents registry that publishes her home
address** (30 Apr) — which she names directly as **doxxing**: "After
Llewellyn Falco doxing me (publishing my home address)... I will block
some people who endorse his shitty behavior because they turn a blind
eye intentionally." She's explicit the underlying relationship was
personal, not just professional — "our breakup (he left)" — but
insists on separating the two: "This is not about the breakup. This is
harassment in the professional community." Consequences she takes
herself: files DMCA takedowns and GDPR removal requests, is banned from
publishing further mobbing-related titles on Leanpub as fallout, leaves
any community that continues to host him ("conferences that will have
him will always get a no from me"), and runs a mass-block script that
ultimately removes ~700 accounts (total ~3452 who can no longer see
her) — a deliberate self-deplatforming: "I know I chose to deplatform
myself blocking those people. They were people who gave me visibility
here." Publishes a general list of what *not* to do when someone
reports harassment (centering yourself, belittling, gaslighting with
"breakups are hard," doubling down, blaming the report) drawn directly
from the responses she receives. The Mob Programming Guidebook itself
keeps shipping regardless — a new edition is published within two weeks
of the dispute, via her own site rather than Leanpub.
