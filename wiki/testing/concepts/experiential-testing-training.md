# Experiential Exploratory Testing Training

A new, sustained teaching practice — distinct from her conference
workshops and from the junior-dev mentoring documented in
[[whole-team-testing]] — built around 1:1 and small-group remote
sessions, pairing on a real (simple) application with testers who have
under five years' experience.

## 2018 Q2 — launch — [[../batches/2018-q2|batch]]

Opens with a public call for participants (12 May): "I'm looking for a
tester with less than 5 years of experience I could practice my teaching
model on. An hour every two weeks for 3-6 months." The response
overwhelms the plan — 40-50 volunteers off one tweet — so she takes
people first-come and schedules through group Calendly setups: "Found
three people from different sides of the world" the same day, then
"Found 20 people... Experiential Exploratory Testing Training my style
will follow."

The first 1:1 session runs 13 May — "We tested a super-simple
application and got to compare approaches" — and the method gets
described directly across several posts as it's worked out in public:
she tests first and asks about the person only afterward ("A note on my
1st session: I test first, ask about the person later. And that is
fascinating"); questions probe the testing before the tester — "What
did we just learn about the application?" then "What did we just learn
about testing?"; pre-session planning is actively discouraged —
"Documenting before testing is a bad idea, folks"; and she names a
distinction between **shallow** exploratory testing (what a trainee does
first) and **deep** (what she starts and ends a session doing) — not
about quality, but depth in layers.

The first group session (18 May) is rougher: a 68% no-show rate, plus
another third of participants ending up in a separate video-hangout
room by accident. She keeps going anyway, running weekly-ish sessions
with participants from Serbia, Portugal, and elsewhere through 19 June,
before opening "a few more group coaching sessions" ahead of a planned
summer break (11 June). Repeatedly frames teaching as two-way, not
one-way delivery — "Always be open to learn while #teaching" — and
reports learning new things about her own small test application from
trainees' fresh eyes.

Launches the same week her [[../../personal/concepts/writing-a-book|Exploratory
Testing Book]] hits its Publish button, and the same month she becomes a
manager (see [[management-transition]]) — three new commitments landing
at once.

## 2018 Q3 — resumes after the summer break — [[../batches/2018-q3|batch]]

Picks back up as planned: "Home internet upgraded from 100M to 1G. I
expect new joy for my #teaching #coaching #testing sessions, about to
continue after summer break." Alongside the volunteer 1:1/small-group
program, runs paid two-day exploratory-testing courses for groups,
experimenting with a specific technique — teaching participants to
*name* things (functionalities, variables) as a way of giving structure
to what they explore — with mixed early results ("worked like charm"
once, "conflicting evidence" the second time), treated as something to
keep experimenting with rather than a settled method.

## 2021 Q1 — Exploratory Testing Foundations, productized — [[../batches/2021-q1|batch]]

The 1:1/small-group practice launched 2018 Q2 gets a formal, reusable
shape: **Exploratory Testing Foundations**, a classroom course (first
delivered 17 Feb, licensed CC BY 4.0) built jointly with a recurring
group of collaborators — @irjastraus, @Parveen_Khan10, and @AdharaIT —
who pair-test the target application ahead of each delivery. The same
content is being built in parallel as a video course, but she notices
mid-delivery that slides written for video don't map cleanly onto
in-class facilitation, and separately that she barely needs slides at
all: "I teach from doing." Rather than keep the material in
presentation-software notes, she folds it into a chapter of her
LeanPub **Exploratory Testing book**, publishing the slide notes as
prose instead — "I was uncomfortable writing all that text in slide
notes in powerpoint, so I made slides and their notes a chapter." The
same four collaborators plan a public follow-on — four
#ExploratoryTestingAcademy sessions in late April, same target
application, each with a different constraint (unconfirmed issues,
test-automation, self-management, and an open Q&A) — extending the
"experiential" method from private coaching into a repeatable public
format for the first time. Separately, at #TCC2021 she agrees with
@profesor_dragan to co-build a **Real-World Exploratory Testing**
course, a second collaboration alongside the Foundations line.
