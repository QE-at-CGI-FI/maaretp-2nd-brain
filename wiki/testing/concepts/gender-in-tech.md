# Gender in Tech/Testing (professional advocacy)

Professional-context gender advocacy — distinct from, but connected to,
her personal experience of gender documented in the personal wiki's
`self-understanding.md`. First named directly in 2013; becomes a
recurring, active throughline in 2014.

## 2014 — [[../batches/2014|batch]]

- Co-authors a position with a colleague: "we need so many brilliant women
  submitting to [[../entities/eurostar|EuroSTAR]] that gender is not a
  criteria" — blogs on it, pushing quality-of-submissions over
  quota-thinking.
- Engages with "female conference speaker bingo" and an article on the
  "insane double standard" women face in tech, particularly around
  personality criticism — "sounds familiar. Then again, my personality is
  flawed but I work on it continuously."
- A real methodological fight in Finnish: challenges a "24% women in IT"
  statistic, pointing out the sample (professional-association membership)
  likely excludes acceptance testers — a role she's personally led teams
  of, "one man, tens of women." Pushes for the real undercount to be fixed
  before it's used to justify interventions.
- Explicitly separates her own agenda from "more women in tech": "more
  women in tech wasn't my agenda at all. But diversity of personalities in
  software development is." Consistent with her not-purely-identity-driven
  framing elsewhere.
- Notices smaller structural signals: conference speaker t-shirts cut only
  for men by default; being thanked as one of "the guys" for things she
  did; a company Christmas coat gift defaulting to a men's cut until she
  points it out (later fixed).
- The advocacy has a personal-life counterpart this year too — thinking
  about "the advice I'd give my daughter on choosing to be involved in
  IT," warnings included.

## 2015 Q1 — [[../batches/2015-q1|batch]]

Moves from commentary to structural participation: becomes a mentor for
**SpeakEasy**, a program pairing new (often underrepresented) speakers
with mentors to develop conference proposals. A small resolved callback
to 2014's t-shirt complaint: TestBash offers both women's and men's cut
conference shirts — "this is me in first ever conf t-shirt," worn as a
notable first specifically because it fit.
