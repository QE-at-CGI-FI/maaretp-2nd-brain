# Gender in Tech/Testing (professional advocacy)

Professional-context gender advocacy — distinct from, but connected to,
her personal experience of gender documented in the personal wiki's
`self-understanding.md`. First named directly in 2013; becomes a
recurring, active throughline in 2014.

## 2014 — [[../batches/2014|batch]]

- Co-authors a position with a colleague: "we need so many brilliant women
  submitting to [[../entities/eurostar|EuroSTAR]] that gender is not a
  criteria" — blogs on it, pushing quality-of-submissions over
  quota-thinking.
- Engages with "female conference speaker bingo" and an article on the
  "insane double standard" women face in tech, particularly around
  personality criticism — "sounds familiar. Then again, my personality is
  flawed but I work on it continuously."
- A real methodological fight in Finnish: challenges a "24% women in IT"
  statistic, pointing out the sample (professional-association membership)
  likely excludes acceptance testers — a role she's personally led teams
  of, "one man, tens of women." Pushes for the real undercount to be fixed
  before it's used to justify interventions.
- Explicitly separates her own agenda from "more women in tech": "more
  women in tech wasn't my agenda at all. But diversity of personalities in
  software development is." Consistent with her not-purely-identity-driven
  framing elsewhere.
- Notices smaller structural signals: conference speaker t-shirts cut only
  for men by default; being thanked as one of "the guys" for things she
  did; a company Christmas coat gift defaulting to a men's cut until she
  points it out (later fixed).
- The advocacy has a personal-life counterpart this year too — thinking
  about "the advice I'd give my daughter on choosing to be involved in
  IT," warnings included.

## 2015 Q1 — [[../batches/2015-q1|batch]]

Moves from commentary to structural participation: becomes a mentor for
**SpeakEasy**, a program pairing new (often underrepresented) speakers
with mentors to develop conference proposals. A small resolved callback
to 2014's t-shirt complaint: TestBash offers both women's and men's cut
conference shirts — "this is me in first ever conf t-shirt," worn as a
notable first specifically because it fit.

## 2015 Q2 — [[../batches/2015-q2|batch]]

A busier quarter for this theme than any since 2014, spanning several
distinct threads:

- A pointed public exchange with an EsConfs organizer over keynote
  selection — "just curious: can you name women who could talk on the
  themes you needed?" — and a broader critique of "submit to pay to
  speak" CFPs as excluding diversity beyond gender.
- Names her own boundary explicitly: "if they emailed me they thought of
  me because of my gender, I might find better use of my time" — declining
  to be chosen *for* her gender, consistent with the 2013 position.
- A formal, public commitment at #Dialogi2015: "I commit to considering
  female experts when looking for e.g. a speaker, commentator or
  consultant."
- Blogs on two women leaving programming in Finland, calling the trend
  "absurd."
- Notices 3 of 10 participants in a Tech Excellence TDD session were
  women and is "impressed... until I realise it could easily be more" —
  catching her own lowered bar in the moment, not just reporting a stat.
- Advocates for women's lightning talks at #AgileTD and helps facilitate.
- A parenting-adjacent version of the same instinct: requesting man-woman
  pairs for [[../entities/teaching-kids-programming|kids' coding
  sessions]] specifically "to model what is right."

## 2015 Q3 — [[../batches/2015-q3|batch]]

The busiest quarter yet for this theme, moving further into structural
action:

- **The Agile2015 t-shirt episode**: women's conference shirts turn out
  to be sized-down, not styled differently — "see the difference? It's
  not in the place you usually need it for" — so she cuts her own shirt
  with scissors to actually fit, blogs about it, and argues "sponsors
  should want women to wear, not use for cleaning."
- Attends #WomenInAgile networking at Agile2015 and sets up an email list
  for it.
- Praises TestBashNY's 50/50 speaker gender split as a direct result of
  Rosie Sherry's community-building work.
- Names a sharper, more specific critique than before: "most women in
  tech movements diss women in testing" — no listing/promoting of
  testing conferences by broader women-in-tech initiatives, a doubled
  marginalization she hadn't named this explicitly before.
- Sets up a **Women Keynoting mastermind group** with @deborahh — a
  second concrete structural program alongside SpeakEasy mentoring.
- At #TAHelsinki, tracks the numbers directly (45% women attendees, only
  3 of 15 speakers) and works the room in real time finding new women
  speakers for future events, offering to write out a list of 30.
- A conference-culture observation distinct from her own identity: not
  drinking alcohol at conferences draws disproportionate attention and
  the awkwardness is gendered — "I used to get 'are you pregnant' a lot.
  Now just apologies I don't need." Blogs a piece collecting "stories and
  thoughts of being a woman (like me) in testing."

## 2015 Q4 — [[../batches/2015-q4|batch]]

Two structural programs and one sharp new observation:

- The **Women Keynoting mastermind group** (planned in Q3) launches for
  real — a paid, facilitated group alongside a free monthly lean-coffee
  session, both aimed at getting women into keynote slots specifically,
  not just speaking slots.
- The all-women **Halloween Game Hack** hackathon: she's accepted,
  notices subtler dynamics even in an all-women space ("three mentions of
  not needing to be technical to set up a website... not said when
  genders are mixed"), and draws a clean line — "all women hackathon = no
  competition. Good."
- Wants to organize a **DJ-ing course for young girls** — "tired of 'AV
  boys' and want to help them run a disco" — extending the same instinct
  from [[../entities/teaching-kids-programming|kids' coding]] into a new
  domain.
- A Finnish Women's Equality Day observation connects gender directly to
  [[pay-to-speak|#PayToSpeak]]'s underlying economics: volunteer
  community work in IT is disproportionately done by women, unpaid —
  "work like that should be compensated."
