# Empirical Research on Testing

A recurring, serious (not casual) interest in studying testing
academically — distinct from her practitioner-community organizing work,
though the two people she discusses it with most are the same:
[[../entities/juha-itkonen]] and [[../entities/michael-bolton]]. Opens
in 2011 with a stated two-year goal to co-author an academic article,
sparked by [[exploratory-testing]]'s dissertation debate with Itkonen;
2012 turns that intent into real, if unpublished, action — mining her
own team's bug data with an explicit qualitative-methods lens, and
drawing a repeated line between practitioner "folklore" and research
held to a higher evidentiary bar. By 2013 the arc shifts from producing
to consuming and connecting — reading, praising others' published work
— without her own publication materializing, and then goes quiet for
over a decade. It resurfaces in 2025, not as another article attempt but
as a formal degree-completion effort: an oral, competency-recognition
route ("one spoken conversation" per course rather than coursework) to
a credential she says she "never did," built on a thesis and framed
explicitly as converting fifteen years of blog/social "grey literature"
into "the academically acceptable." By 2026 that framing recurs as a
named, ongoing project rather than a finished one — the 2011 ambition's
underlying goal (having her practitioner knowledge count as legitimate
in academic terms) finally gets sustained action, though the archive
doesn't confirm whether the 2025 degree itself was completed.

## 2011 — [[../batches/2011|batch]]

Origin point: the week-long critique/defense of [[../entities/juha-itkonen]]'s
PhD dissertation (see [[exploratory-testing]]). States a goal directly:
"in the next two years, I want to write an academic article. I'll should
have a co-author from university research group."

## 2012 — [[../batches/2012|batch]]

Moves from intent to action:
- Itkonen agrees to help her write up her latest project for an academic
  quality-research audience.
- Attends an ESPA research seminar summarizing recent Finnish testing
  research; watches Itkonen present on test oracles — "this was the last
  one in his dissertation."
- Starts mining her own team's repository/bug data — hours logged to fix
  bugs, by whom, with what pattern — looking for a defensible way to show
  the contribution of a tester like her, not just raw bug counts. Explicit
  about method: "I believe this must be mostly founded in qualitative
  research methods. But some quantitative stuff would be useful." Orders a
  book on qualitative research methods on Bolton's recommendation.
- Skeptical consumer, not just producer, of testing research: dismisses
  a "crowd size and time restriction" study for treating testing as a
  single task rather than a collection of different activities, and
  objects on Twitter to "anecdotes from what someone claimed" being
  presented as facts, calling it "folklore" — a repeated distinction she
  draws between practitioner folklore (useful, but not evidence) and
  research (which she holds to a higher bar even when she likes the
  conclusion).

## 2013 — [[../batches/2013|batch]]

Less hands-on data mining this year, more consumption and connection:
reads a social-sciences research methods book Bolton recommended, praises
a blog post applying qualitative-research concepts to testing, and
celebrates [[../entities/juha-itkonen|Itkonen]] and Mäntylä's ESEJ paper
"Are Test Cases Needed?" getting accepted. The academic-article ambition
from 2011 hasn't produced her own publication yet, but the surrounding
research literacy keeps deepening.

## 2025 — a real degree attempt, twelve years after the article ambition — [[../batches/2025|batch]]

In April, a genuinely new move on the old ambition: "Today I became a
student and I will graduate (which I never did) after one spoken
conversation in Swedish and a thesis work with lower academic standards
than my publications. I already started the thesis and expect to
graduate in June." The reaction is mixed rather than triumphant —
"happy that schools know how to score the budget they need, without
lowering the bar they have," but also "awful on switch from uni to
vocational" and irritated that customers weight the credential in bids.
By May the format itself becomes a small finding of its own: "studying
is taking a private teaching form in this program... they call it
'showing your knowledge,' but when your knowledge has gaps, I call it
'private teaching'" — three courses in, she's still learning real gaps
(terminology, CSR messaging) from 30-minute conversations she'd have
hated as lectures. In August, the older 2011–2013 thread's real subject
surfaces directly, independent of the degree itself: "academic writing
style is so hard for me as my references are, what they like to say,
grey sources... most of us practitioners write grey sources" and
"feeling inappropriate while intentionally whitening my grey
literature... so much of knowledge of testing is unavailable for the
research world" — explaining her own reasoning about exploratory
testing to "a university teacher I charge of my thesis," and noting the
irony that she's having, for the first time successfully, a conversation
she'd failed to land 20 years earlier. No confirmation in the archive of
whether the June graduation target was met.

## 2026 — the same reframe, named as a standing project — [[../batches/2026|batch]]

A single callback in early January restates the 2025 thesis-year
reasoning as an ongoing, harder-than-expected project rather than a
closed one: "15 years of blogging and creating grey literature. To
change the world, I need to move what is grey to the academically
acceptable. I started this early last fall, but the more I work on it,
the more I recognize I have work cut out for me." No further mention in
the range ingested (through August 2026).
