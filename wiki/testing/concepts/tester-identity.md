# Tester Identity, Not Role

A distinction she insists on repeatedly through 2014: "I find no reason to
give up my tester identity... It's not my role, it's my identity."

## 2014 — [[../batches/2014|batch]]

The pressure comes from the agile community itself, not from testing
skeptics: colleagues and peers suggest she drop "tester" for "developer"
or "software professional" to avoid silos. She refuses, framing identity
as load-bearing for her happiness: "Call me a developer and I'm done with
sw." She experiments with alternate labels (software specialist,
implementer-vs-developer as a pay/skill distinction) but keeps returning
to tester as non-negotiable.

This distinction becomes the flashpoint for the year's most significant
relational event: a long, escalating Twitter exchange with
[[../entities/james-bach|James Bach]] (26–27 Sept) over whether "role" is
a useful concept at all. She argues role is assigned and she'd rather
craft her contribution from activities and skills; he reads this as an
attack on a concept central to his framework and says he's upset. She
tries repeatedly to de-escalate ("are you trying to understand me or just
prove me wrong?") and eventually needs to step away. The next morning, she
discovers he's **blocked her on Twitter** — with no explanation offered
either then or later in the archive. She calls the block itself, not the
disagreement, "really bad habits," and seriously considers whether she
wants to stay associated with the CDT community over it, before deciding
community isn't one person and staying. See [[../entities/james-bach]].

## 2015 Q1 — [[../batches/2015-q1|batch]]

The identity fight becomes an explicit vocabulary experiment. Frustrated
at "you are not a tester" reactions from people who only know
commodity/factory-school testers, she starts introducing herself as
"exploratory tester" instead — a narrower label that gets a different,
more receptive reaction than "tester" alone. Frames the underlying
distinction as commodity vs. skilled testing, not tester vs. developer.
Pushes back publicly on the "just call them business analysts / drop
'tester' from the title" pattern she sees spreading at TestBash: "just
having the word tester in your job title downplays your skills... any
other way to fix than renaming us?"

Engages, cautiously, with [[../entities/james-bach|Bach]] and
[[../entities/michael-bolton|Bolton]]'s "Exploratory Testing 3.0" /
"Tester 3.0" framing — tags both directly in conversation about why they
chose "checking" over renaming testing itself, and separately blogs that
she's "not ready for Exploratory Testing 3.0." Notable given the Sept 2014
block: the direct @-mentions suggest either the block lifted or she's
willing to keep engaging with his public work regardless. Not confirmed
either way in the tweets themselves — worth watching in later batches.

## 2015 Q2 — [[../batches/2015-q2|batch]]

Sharpest statement yet of the identity-not-role distinction, in a thread
with @SmartGoogler: developers can test (reframes "developer changes the
code, introduces a new bug, she tests the fix" to show testing isn't
gatekept), testers can fix production code without stopping being
testers, and the line she draws isn't around tasks at all — "I draw the
line with my *identity* not the tasks I do." Also, in the parallel
[[../entities/michael-bolton|Bolton]] debate: "I don't see the gatekeeper
role metaphor as the right one to consider... goalies _can_ leave the
goal," and "I prefer to view the world as a place where I'm blocked by my
(lack of) skills, not my role." Consistent with 2014's formulation but now
applied specifically to the boundary question — what a tester is and isn't
"allowed" to do — rather than the label itself.
