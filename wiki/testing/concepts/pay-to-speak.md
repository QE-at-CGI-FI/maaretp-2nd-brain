# #PayToSpeak

A sustained advocacy campaign, not just personal grumbling: she names and
tracks which conferences require speakers to pay their own way (entry,
travel, stay) versus which compensate them, and pushes publicly for the
latter to become the norm.

## 2015 Q3 — [[../batches/2015-q3|batch]]

Runs through the whole quarter. Starts from her own math — "try doing 10
conferences a year, it costs a lot," self-funded, her employer doesn't
cover it — and generalizes into structural critique: conferences that
don't pay speakers still often refuse them free workshop entry, and she
names three tiers she's willing to accept (entry / entry+travel-stay /
entry+travel-stay+honorarium), crediting AlterConf for shifting her
thinking. Collects her own experiences into a spreadsheet ("mistakes are
mine, things may change") and repeatedly names the conferences breaking
the pattern in a good way: #AgileTD, #TestBash, #EuroTestConf, and later
Copenhagen Context. Distinct from, but adjacent to, her
[[../entities/international-speaking|international speaking]] costs and
her [[gender-in-tech]] advocacy — the throughline is fairness to
speakers generally, not compensation for her specifically.
