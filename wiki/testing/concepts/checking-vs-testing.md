# Checking vs. Testing

## 2010 — [[../batches/2010|batch]]

Worked out largely in public, in reply threads with [[james-bach]] and
[[michael-bolton]] — this is the context-driven testing community's
checking/testing distinction taking shape in real time, and she's actively
negotiating her own vocabulary rather than just adopting theirs.

- Prefers "confirmatory" for what Bach/Bolton were starting to call
  "checking": "confirmatory is the word I use on what you seem to have
  called checking lately."
- Pushes on the edges of "negative testing" — argues it's really a mixture
  of error handling and non-confirmatory testing, and that the term doesn't
  describe intent well: "other words would describe the intent better."
- Coins/borrows "demonstration magic" as a phrase she likes better than
  checking-vs-testing for describing a particular failure mode.
- Michael Bolton's line, quoted approvingly: "I test. You observe. We talk.
  You decide." — reads as a proto-definition of the tester's role that
  resonates with her.

## 2011 — [[../batches/2011|batch]]

Less of a direct thread this year — the vocabulary work migrates into the
bigger [[exploratory-testing]] debate around [[../entities/juha-itkonen]]'s
dissertation (what does and doesn't count as ET, rather than
checking-vs-testing specifically). No major new terminology adopted; she
stays more interested in whether a term helps people test better than in
getting the term itself exactly right.

## 2012 — [[../batches/2012|batch]]

Position settles rather than shifts: "I prefer to think checking as part
of testing. However, the problem I see often that for many, there's no
more than the check." And, more strongly: "all testing is exploratory. The
checks are result of exploration too, just a different type." Checking
isn't opposed to testing or to exploration in her framing anymore — it's a
subset of both.

## 2013 — [[../batches/2013|batch]]

Crystallizes into a formula she repeats and defends: "Testing = confirmed +
investigated = checked + explored." And, more precisely: "I know, I don't
even check. I know but I'm uncertain, I check. I know I don't know and
need to learn, I explore." — three distinct epistemic states, not a
checking/testing binary. She defends this framing directly against
[[../entities/james-bach|James Bach]] after his "Testing and Checking
Refined" post: rather than "attacking" the checking-vs-testing distinction,
she offers a model where the two are "not vs but two sides," arguing his
RST students often walk away remembering the versus and losing the nuance.
This is the most sustained, structured disagreement she has with Bach in
the whole archive — see [[../entities/james-bach]] and
[[../batches/2013]].
