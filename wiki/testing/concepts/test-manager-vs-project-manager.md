# Test Manager vs. Project Manager

## 2010 — [[../batches/2010|batch]]

A recurring identity/role tension, distinct from the documentation fight but
entangled with it: where does test management end and project management
begin, and does she want to be pulled toward the latter?

- "Can't overcome my crisis with differences of project and test manager.
  Managing expert testers does not need test emphasis in mgmt."
- Objects to test managers being made responsible for bug-fix coordination
  rather than just surfacing bugs with good information — "No time for
  testing."
- Wants a "positive approach to get away from being trained (again) as
  project manager when I just want to be a tester."
- Skeptical of the "test manager" title itself when applied loosely: "what's
  a _test_ manager who has 20+ years of experience being a project manager
  and 2 with test."
- Not purely negative about management — later in the year describes her
  role as "a test manager with good-enough networks to actually change
  things," suggesting she's finding a version of the role she can live with
  by year's end.

## 2011 — [[../batches/2011|batch]]

The tension resolves into an actual role change, twice over. A new test
manager joins her project in December, freeing her to do non-management
work — "so much fun." Then, days later: "turned to dark side. Project
manager for a couple of months. I hope I make my way back to testing..."
She frames test management as accidentally good practice for PM work
("test management made pretty good practice for project mgmt") even while
resenting the pull toward it. Earlier in the year: "test management is too
much like project management. With weak pm, I'm so close to moving to the
dark side: no testing anymore" — so the December move reads as the
tension finally winning, not a new development.

## 2012 — resolution — [[../batches/2012|batch]]

The multi-year tension resolves. Early in the year, as PM, she can't tell
the difference from being test manager "except for wider audience" — the
roles really had converged for her, as she'd predicted in 2011. Then she
quits [[../entities/ilmarinen|Ilmarinen]] and takes a hands-on tester +
test automation role instead: "from one dark side to another. From being a
project manager to being a test automation developer." By April: "I feel
like a tester again - just what I needed. Test management is just not
testing. I love my new work." Looking back on the whole arc in October,
she reframes it not as a detour but as training: "I've moved up to be a
better tester. Needed to sidetrack in management though, but now back." —
management was instrumental to becoming a better tester, not a competing
identity to escape.

She's also newly blunt that this was never really about ambition: when
asked whether "test manager" was a step toward something like CFO, her
answer is that it wouldn't even be a promotion, salary or otherwise — "just
be a good tester."
