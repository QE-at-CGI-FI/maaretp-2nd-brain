# 2010

224 posts, 2010-05-13 – 2010-12-31 (the archive's earliest surviving tweets — account presumably started mid-May). All professional; nothing personal in this batch beyond a passing "weekends are reserved for family."

## Context

A career transition is visible across the year: she moves from a previous
workplace where [[exploratory-testing]] was already established practice,
into testing on a pension-insurance data-intensive system — at
[[../entities/ilmarinen|Ilmarinen]], though the archive doesn't name the
employer until 2012, on her way out — in a sector she repeatedly describes
as plan- and document-driven, working with subcontractors. By late
December: "after 16 months of testing work in a new company, first
acceptance test completed."

## What's notable

- **Pushing exploratory testing uphill.** The dominant thread all year: trying
  to introduce [[exploratory-testing]] into a sector/company that doesn't do
  it, meeting real resistance ("sector-wise not ready... will do in one
  company only"), and defending it against the assumption that ET needs a
  UI or is unstructured.
- **Distrust of documentation as a deliverable.** Recurring frustration with
  test cases and test plans as *documents to be graded* rather than useful
  artifacts — "correct but useless," "amazing how much people are willing to
  pay for useless documentation," wanting to throw out an accepted test plan.
  See [[documentation-skepticism]].
- **Working out vocabulary in public, with the people who coined it.** Long
  reply threads with [[james-bach]] and [[michael-bolton]] hashing out
  negative testing / non-confirmatory testing / "demonstration magic" and
  the checking-vs-testing split — this is the context-driven testing
  community's terminology taking shape in real time. See
  [[checking-vs-testing]].
- **Test manager vs. project manager tension.** Repeated confusion/friction
  about where the test manager role ends and the PM role begins, and
  discomfort at being pulled toward PM-style work. See
  [[test-manager-vs-project-manager]].
- **Building testing community in Finland**, in Finnish specifically —
  organizing seminars and a testing dojo, running BBST, weighing a Finnish
  translation of a well-known cheat sheet, torn throughout the year between
  writing in Finnish vs. English. See [[ast-bbst]],
  [[weekend-testing-and-dojos]].
- **Quality Center** used and consistently distrusted as a test management
  tool; several posts prefer Excel to it. See [[quality-center]].
- Runs her own Finnish-language materials site, testauskirja.com. See
  [[testauskirja]].
