# 2018 Q3

1513 posts total, 1112 after dropping retweets, 2018-07-01 – 2018-09-30
(Twitter only). A dense quarter, dominated by a career decision that,
unlike similar ones in 2015–2017, actually holds: she retires from
unpaid conference speaking.

## What's notable

- **[[../entities/international-speaking|International speaking]]**:
  "retired keynote speaker (until further notice)" from mid-July, and it
  sticks for the rest of the quarter — driven by a sharp attribution
  conflict with Richard Bradshaw (FriendlyTester) in early July and a
  raw, newly surfaced account of how harmful Agile2015/2016 were for her
  personally as a tester ("I barely survived... anxiety attacks"). By
  late September she nuances the retirement: paid training courses stay,
  and one January 2019 conference gets accepted.
- **[[../entities/eurotestingconf|EuroTestConf]] 2019** scales up hugely:
  311 submissions (+60% year over year), roughly 35% from non-men, 200+
  hours of collaboration calls, two full scholarships, and a spun-off
  peer exploratory-testing workshop planned for Valencia.
- **[[../concepts/management-transition|Management transition]]** turns
  a real corner: she starts actively enjoying the role ("I've done magic
  being just myself now that I'm an engineering manager"), kills a
  two-week daily-standup experiment that wasn't working, and resumes her
  long-paused Computer Science degree.
- **[[../concepts/exploratory-testing|Exploratory testing]]**: a
  prolific writing stretch — close to a dozen blog posts refining the
  concept ("low quality exploratory testing," "the line between
  exploratory testing and managing it," "Three Kinds of Testing").
- **[[../concepts/gender-in-tech|Gender in tech]]**: continuing
  mansplaining/CoC skepticism, a sharp new line — "white male is the
  lowest difficulty setting I never get to play on" — and a "too
  emotional" feedback incident after 25 years of professional
  experience.
