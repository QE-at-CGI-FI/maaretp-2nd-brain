# Self-Understanding

## 2011 — [[../batches/2011|batch]]

First real seam of self-reflection in the archive, mostly triggered by
pairing/collaboration friction at work rather than introspection for its
own sake:

- **MBTI**: typed as ENFP (first mentioned in the 2010 testing batch, in a
  professional context). In 2011 she engages with the type more personally
  — notes a real tension between being classified as an extrovert and her
  "need for self-clarification time," which she's "still working through."
  Separately says MBTI knowledge helps her "respect people I don't like
  because we're just so different," framing it as a coping tool for
  workplace friction rather than pure self-interest.
- **Being "intimidating."** A one-line, mostly-joking aside that still
  reads as a real self-perception: "Someone should teach me how to be
  myself and not intimidating. I need more intimidating people around me
  not to stand out so much." Worth watching whether this recurs.
- **Learning style**: "I don't work well without preparation in a meeting
  of 'let's write a plan together.' I think by writing, and need to
  restructure the text" — and later, "I'm semi-useless in meetings where I
  can't work on whiteboard or pre-prepare by solo-writing." A consistent,
  named preference for processing alone/in writing before group work.

## 2014 — [[../batches/2014|batch]]

- **MBTI, retested and stable**: "whenever I do myers-briggs with
  different questions, I'm sure I would end up introvert but always:
  ENFP." Explains the apparent contradiction directly: "professional me
  tends to be very different from private me. And real extroverts are
  more comfortable socializing" — the clearest statement yet of a
  persistent public/private split she's alluded to since 2011.
- **"Confessions from a bully"** — her "most personal blog post to date,"
  triggered by a conversation about being nice vs. mean online. The tweets
  don't say what the underlying incident was, only that writing about it
  "taught me something about me," and that she asked to be read kindly.
  Not further explained or revisited in the rest of the batch.
- **A workplace sexist incident, named and acted on.** A team member
  responds to her suggestion of mob programming for introverts with a
  comment about "big-breasted women" in a team meeting. She's visibly
  upset across several tweets, works through possible explanations (fear,
  a deflection habit), and reports raising it formally with her manager —
  unlike the 2013 "unfit for my profession" disclosure, this one has a
  named response, not just a private wound.
- **Fear of pairing/programming, named without euphemism**: "way out of my
  comfort zone... pairing terrifies me," and later, asking other testers
  if they share "irrational fears related to that." Ties to the
  professional-side account in
  `../../testing/concepts/whole-team-testing.md`.
- A softer note in December: her employer's Christmas gift (winter coats)
  defaults to a women's cut for her without her having to ask — "as
  female minority, I REALLY appreciate that."

## 2018 Q2 — a low point taking feedback, and a moment of self-recognition — [[../batches/2018-q2|batch]]

being on the receiving end of criticism:

  everyone." Her first reaction is close to despair — "I feel there is

  naming her own reflexive wariness (built from years of being talked
  over by men, see `../../testing/concepts/gender-in-tech.md`) as
  something that can misfire onto someone who didn't deserve it.

## 2019 Q2 — turning 45, an old isolation memory, and a recurring helper fantasy — [[../batches/2019-q2|batch]]

just nostalgic. Also begins **Patreon** this quarter — "back to
blogging... experiment with new ways of feeling sense of connectedness"

self-published books tracked in `writing-a-book.md`, and consistent with
