# Health & Accommodation Needs

A severe animal (dog) allergy, named for the first time in the archive
this quarter, with real professional and family cost attached — distinct
from the disability/accessibility advocacy she does *for others* (see
`../../testing/concepts/gender-in-tech.md` and elsewhere), this is a
personal accommodation need of her own.

## 2019 Q2 — a conference conflict, a workplace conflict, and a skipped family gathering — [[../batches/2019-q2|batch]]

Three separate incidents in one quarter establish the severity plainly:

- **TestBash's conference dog** (mid-April): "a meeting with dog owners
  is two days of explaining I'm not sick with a flu. A touch on bare
  skin is burning red marks. Time in the room is a hospital visit if I'm
  not smart enough to escape" — severe enough that she names not
  attending as the only real option: "dogs in, me out." Distinguishes
  her objection carefully from disability accommodation, which she
  actively supports: "I'm a deep believer in making space for those who
  need service dogs" — and discloses that this exact tension already
  cost her something at **her own conference**: she came close to
  excluding herself from it over a service dog, resolved only by paying
  for a human assistant instead.
- **A workplace exposure** (22 May): a colleague's new dog makes even a
  private meeting impossible — "watery eyes and inability to stop
  coughing. A one hour meeting causes 2 days of 'flu'" — compounded by
  how closely the colleague keeps the animal's fur/scent on their
  clothes at work. Names the accommodation gap half-jokingly but not
  lightly: "we're between building a decontamination unit on the team
  room door before I move in and me wearing a gas mask."
- **A family gathering skipped** (21 June): the whole extended family
  meets at a venue with animals present — "so my choice is I have no
  choice but not to go" — the same condition costing her time with
  family, not just work access.

Read together, this is a recurring, unresolved practical constraint on
where she can physically be — conferences, workplace, and family
gatherings alike — not a one-off complaint.

## 2022 Q4 — a new, quieter accommodation: degrading eyesight — [[../batches/2022-q4|batch]]

A second, unrelated accommodation surfaces for the first time,
disclosed matter-of-factly rather than as a crisis: "My eyesight has
been degrading, and I find a lot of comfort in a few skills I picked up
already while I could see well" — touch-typing, contributing to pairs/
ensembles without touching the keyboard, working remotely for control
over text size, and starting to integrate text-to-speech. Framed with
the same dry acceptance as the allergy entries on this page: "with the
odd chance of becoming old(er)... Computer work relies on eyes a lot."
